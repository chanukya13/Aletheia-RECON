# Public evidence from 15 winning hackathon editions

Reviewed 8 November 2026 for technical product design and evaluation. This is a convenience sample of 15 editions from seven competition series. Named category awards and overall awards are identified separately. Public project descriptions are author-reported evidence. Repository and demo links show what was offered for inspection, rather than proving that every linked system still runs. Current pages can differ from the versions judges saw. Missing public test details are recorded as unavailable.

## Findings applied to RECON

The richer project pages generally make the same questions easy to inspect: the problem and intended user, what the product does, how it was built, difficult implementation points, working demonstration and source links, and what remains to be done. A screenshot or architecture description is evidence of what the authors presented; an accessible repository link is still short of an independently reproduced result. The NASA and Stanford organizer accounts provide less project-level detail than the full showcases, as noted above. This review records both evidence levels rather than filling their gaps with assumptions.

These examples support a product design judgment, rather than a causal claim about how to win. Repeatedly visible elements include a concrete user problem, a recognizable input/output workflow, identifiable data or integrations, and a way to inspect a demonstration or source artifact. Google explicitly lists functionality, purpose, content, user experience and technical execution in its Chrome judging accounts.

The public evidence does not support a rule that winners must claim a percentage improvement. Several submissions discuss evaluation techniques or implementation challenges while omitting the information needed to reproduce a comparative score. This review did not run their repositories, test their live services or inspect private judging evidence. It cannot rank their actual scientific rigor.

For RECON, technical packaging means a coherent investigation, an obvious next action, source-cell and passage inspection, reproducible downloads, and a compact results page tied to executed artifacts. A code-test count measures software regression coverage. A source-cell benchmark measures a narrower data-location task. Neither becomes clinical or historical-identity accuracy.

The current work therefore adds a guided source → interpretation → experiment → audit flow, a source-integrity record owned by the automated assistant, and an executed comparison of lexical retrieval, BM25, metadata-filtered BM25 and structured source-cell lookup. Any ties or failures remain in the report. The scientific review status identifies the reviewer type and the scope of the check, with no additional user approval needed to use the completed software.
